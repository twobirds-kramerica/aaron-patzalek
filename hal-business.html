Why It's Built This Way

Sovereign by design: no single vendor can hold this hostage

Most software gets built on top of someone else's platform, which means its cost, its uptime, and its future all quietly depend on that platform's roadmap and pricing. This one is built the opposite way. Before any tool, service, or dependency is allowed into the stack, it has to answer one question: if this disappeared tomorrow, what breaks? If the honest answer is "a lot," it doesn't get in — a sovereign alternative gets built or found instead.

In practice, that means the system is headless: no single piece is load-bearing for everything else. A tool can be swapped, a vendor can walk away, a pricing model can change, and the rest keeps running. That's not a technical preference — it's what makes the cost and the delivery timeline something a client can actually rely on, instead of something that quietly rides on one company's decisions.
