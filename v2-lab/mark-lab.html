Brand mark — three options

Same rules in all three: Two Birds Blue, the correct fraternal chevrons, wordmark on top, stacked, everything slides in, and a border beam that runs once on arrival then takes a quiet lap every forty seconds. No vertical rule anywhere.

Tagline treatments

Why not emphasised initials: Service, Innovation, Simplified starts S, I, S. Any treatment that highlights first letters builds an implied acronym, and people assemble it whether you meant them to or not. SIS reads as "sis", or as MI6. Three good words end up carrying a fourth meaning you did not choose. The treatments below add rhythm without spelling anything.

1 — Payoff weight (recommended)

Service. Innovation. Simplified.

The last word is the promise; the first two are the setup. Standard practice for a three-beat line is to let the payoff carry the weight. Nothing spells out.
2 — Blue periods

Service. Innovation. Simplified.

Words stay uniform; the punctuation does the brand work. Quiet and confident. Reads well small, which matters at favicon and footer sizes.
3 — Interpuncts, no periods

Service · Innovation · Simplified

Lighter and more modern, but it drops the full stops. That means amending the locked guideline and your live LinkedIn page, not just this site.
4 — Stacked

Service.
Innovation.
Simplified.

Strong in a tall lockup and gives each word its own beat. Competes with the wordmark above it in the plate, so better on a banner than in the hero.
