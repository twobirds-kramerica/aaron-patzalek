# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences arriving at different stages:

1. **Prospective consulting clients** — SME business owners in Southwestern Ontario, considering hiring Aaron for process improvement or AI adoption work. They've clicked from LinkedIn, a referral, or a cold email. They're evaluating: is this person credible, is this for me, how do I start?

2. **Prospective employers / talent scouts** — hiring managers or recruiters looking at Aaron as a senior PM or VP of Product candidate. They're scanning for evidence of work, not just credentials.

Primary task for audience 1: read the consulting offer, understand what they'd get, book a discovery call.
Primary task for audience 2: see real products built, confirm depth of PM experience, move to a conversation.

## Product Purpose

Personal brand and consulting offer page for Aaron Patzalek — founder of Two Birds Innovation, former TELUS senior PM, based in St. Thomas Ontario. The site must serve both audiences without confusing either. The consulting offer is the primary commercial path; the portfolio is the evidence that backs it.

Success looks like: a business owner books a 30-minute discovery call, or a hiring manager initiates a conversation, within 5 minutes of landing on the site.

## Brand Personality

Senior colleague. Direct. No corporate filler.

Tone: plain, confident, non-promotional. The language of someone who has done this for 20 years and doesn't need to oversell. "Here's what I've built" not "I'm passionate about disrupting paradigms."
Voice: warm but direct. Canadian. No em dashes. No buzzwords. "I build tools that work, for people who need them."

## Anti-references

- Guru/coach sites (Tony Robbins aesthetic, motivational language, stock photos of handshakes)
- Agency portfolio sites (too polished, feels like they're hiding the actual person)
- LinkedIn-style resume pages (list of titles and dates — no personality, no story)
- Anything with animated particles, gradient mesh backgrounds, or auto-playing video
- "I help [audience] achieve [transformation] through [methodology]" hero copy — immediate distrust signal

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't pitch.** Four live products with links beat any amount of copy about capabilities.
2. **One entry point per audience.** The consulting CTA and the portfolio evidence should not fight for attention on the same screen.
3. **Earn trust in order.** Problem → credentials → offer → action. Not: award-winning consultant with 20 years of experience (first paragraph).
4. **No stock photography.** The headshot is real, the context is real. Any other image must be a product screenshot or nothing.
5. **The site is a senior PM's PM.** It should demonstrate the same quality bar Aaron applies to products he builds for clients.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum
- Mobile: many prospective clients are viewing on phones, often during a break
- Heading hierarchy must support screen readers — this is a credibility signal as much as an accessibility one
- Font: Inter (currently used) — acceptable but generic. Consider a more distinctive pairing for the display headings.
